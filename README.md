# Northstar — AML Alert Autopsy

**What actually happens when an AML alert lands?**

Northstar is researching how transaction-monitoring alerts are actually investigated in practice — from the moment an alert arrives to the moment an investigator closes it.

Not the policy version.
Not a vendor demo.
Not what we assume happens.

**The real workflow.**

## The Research

We're trying to understand the complete investigation journey:

1. The alert arrives
2. Initial review
3. Context gathering
4. Reconstructing the activity
5. Finding and verifying evidence
6. Reaching a decision
7. Writing the rationale
8. Review and closure

At each stage, we're looking for what actually happens, where information comes from, what investigators have to do manually, and where the process becomes difficult or time-consuming.

## The Fracture Question

We are deliberately **not assuming the problem**.

Possible areas we are investigating include:

* Information scattered across systems
* Context taking time to assemble
* Transaction reconstruction
* Finding previous investigation context
* Missing or contradictory information
* Evidence verification and assembly
* Investigation rationale writing
* Reviewer rework

These are hypotheses, not conclusions.

The evidence comes first.

## What We're Looking For

If you investigate transaction-monitoring alerts, we're interested in hearing about **one real investigation workflow**.

No customer names.
No account numbers.
No confidential case information.

Just the workflow.

**What happened from the moment the alert arrived until it was closed?**

And:

> **Where is our map wrong?**

The goal is to understand what actually makes AML investigations take longer, require more work, or become harder than they appear from the outside.
